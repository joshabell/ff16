Notice of Race & Sailing Instructions

Cape Cod Frosty Fleet #16

2026–2027 Season · Beverly, Massachusetts

1

Event

Cape Cod Frosty Fleet #16 (FF16) organizes weekend races of Cape Cod Frosty Class boats in Beverly, Massachusetts.

This Notice of Race (NoR) covers the 2026–2027 season.

2

Rules

FF16 follows the rules of the Cape Cod Frosty Class Association (CCFCA), with the following modifications and additions:

  1. AdditionIn addition to the required life jacket, dry suits are required unless explicitly waived for a given race by the Race and Safety Committee.
  2. ModificationThe minimum combined weight of hull and crew is voluntary and not required.

For full CCFCA rules, see capecodfrosty.org/rules.htm.

3

Eligibility

All are invited to race. Racers without a Cape Cod Frosty are encouraged to contact the Fleet Captain, who may be able to arrange a loaner boat.

4

Registration

No registration is required. New racers are encouraged to contact the Fleet Captain to be added to race-day venue updates.

5

Fees

To encourage participation, no fee is required from racers who take part in fewer than 3 races.

Racers who take part in 3 or more races pay $25 to the Treasurer. Funds support season prizes, safety-boat fuel and upkeep, and other expenses approved by the Treasurer.

6

Schedule of Races

We aim to race every other week, so racers can also sail with Frosty Fleet #9. The schedule targets 12 races across the season.

Races start at either 11:00 AM or 1:00 PM, whichever is closer to high tide.

BYE weeks occur when both start times fall more than 4 hours from high tide, or when the tide can't hold at least 3 ft for the full 2-hour race.

BYE OR [time] weeks have workable tidal conditions but default to BYE unless the conditions in that row's Notes are met.

Prizes

Trophies are awarded for first through third place, based on scoring for the season.

9

Scoring

FF16 uses a high-point scoring system: every finisher earns points for the size of the fleet they raced in, plus one point for each boat they beat.

Score for a race (Total number of racers) + (Number of racers who finish behind you)

In a race with 8 boats, 1st place beats 7 boats and scores 8 + 7 = 15. 2nd place beats 6 and scores 8 + 6 = 14, and so on down to last place, which beats 0 boats but still scores 8 + 0 = 8 — one point for every boat in the race. A boat that does not race (DNR) scores 0 points for that race, since it never finishes.

Each racer's lowest-scoring 20% of races that season are dropped (rounded down to the nearest whole race). A racer's season total is the sum of their remaining scores.

Example

Alex sails in 9 of a 10-race season and DNRs one race. Since 20% of 10 races is 2, Alex's 2 lowest scores are dropped before the season total is added up — the DNR (0 points) is automatically one of them:

Race12345678910
Alex's score 131017 81415 DNR (0)17912
Season total 13 + 10 + 17 + 14 + 15 + 17 + 9 + 12 (races 4 and 7 dropped) 107

Alex's DNR doesn't hurt the season total at all here — it's simply one of the two throwouts, alongside the other low score of 8.
